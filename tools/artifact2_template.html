Corners
first

1974 年 Ernő Rubik 第一次解開他自己發明的方塊，用的是先解角。人類這樣解是為了好記。 但同一個拆法在機器那邊有完全不同的用途——它是一把量尺。 有了它，上一篇說「量不出來」的東西，這一篇量出來了—— 包括一件反直覺的事：同樣先解角，對人是省事，對機器是繞路，而且繞多遠算得出來。

When Ernő Rubik first solved his own cube in 1974, he solved the corners first. People do it that way because it is easier to remember. The same decomposition has an entirely different use on the machine side — it is a ruler. With it, the thing the previous piece called unmeasurable becomes measurable — including something counter-intuitive: the same decomposition that saves a person effort costs a machine moves, and now we can say exactly how many.

人為什麼先解角

Why people solve corners first

因為八個角是「少而硬」的那一半：只有 8 顆，但每一顆有三個面，位置和朝向都要對。 先把它們固定下來，剩下的十二條邊就變成一個比較單純的問題。 這一派叫 corners-first，1980 年代很流行；1981 年 Marc Waterman 和 Daan Krammer 的 Waterman method 是純 CF 的代表作，發展出上百條解邊的手順和完整的 CLL。

Because the eight corners are the small, awkward half: only eight pieces, but each has three faces, and both position and twist have to be right. Pin them down and the twelve edges become a simpler problem. The family is called corners-first; it was popular through the 1980s, and the 1981 Waterman method by Marc Waterman and Daan Krammer is its most thorough example, with hundreds of edge algorithms and full CLL.

重點是人類這樣拆的理由是記憶，不是效率。先解角會讓總步數變多—— 這一頁後面會把那個代價量出來。人腦沒有辦法一次考慮四千三百二十五京個局面， 只好把問題切成幾塊、每塊背幾十條手順。

The point is that people decompose for memorability, not efficiency. Corners-first makes the solution longer — this page measures exactly how much longer. A human cannot hold 43 quintillion states in mind, so the problem gets cut into pieces and each piece gets a few dozen memorised sequences.

機器也是這樣拆的，但用途相反

Machines cut it the same way — for the opposite reason

Richard Korf 1997 年第一次算出隨機方塊的最短解，用的方法是把方塊 投影到三個較小的子問題上，每個都整個窮舉存成一張表：角塊、六條邊、另外六條邊。 這三張表叫 pattern database。每一張都給出「只解這一部分要幾步」， 而那個數字是「解開整顆要幾步」的下界——因為解開整顆的每一步也都在動這一部分。 三個下界取最大的，就是一個保證不高估的 heuristic。

Richard Korf's 1997 optimal solver projects the cube onto three smaller subproblems and enumerates each one exhaustively: the corners, six edges, and the other six. Those tables are pattern databases. Each says how many moves that part alone needs, and that count is a lower bound on solving the whole cube, because every move of a full solution also moves that part. Take the largest of the three and you have a heuristic that provably never over-estimates.

更早的 Thistlethwaite（1981）則是把方塊拆成四個嵌套的子群，一階一階往裡走， 原始上界 52 步，後來把每一階窮舉之後降到 45 步。

Earlier still, Thistlethwaite (1981) walks the cube down a chain of four nested subgroups, one stage at a time — 52 moves in the original bound, later reduced to 45 by exhaustively searching each stage.

同一個直覺，兩種用途

One intuition, two uses

人類把「先解角」當成記憶的單位：這一段我背得起來。 機器把它當成下界的來源：這一段我數得完，所以我知道答案至少是多少。 這一頁做的就是後者——而做完之後才發現，它順手也能拿來檢驗前者。

A human treats "corners first" as a unit of memory: this part I can hold in my head. A machine treats it as a source of lower bounds: this part I can count, so I know the answer is at least this much. This page does the second — and having done it, discovers it can also be used to check the first.

把角塊整個數完

Counting every corner state

八顆角有 種狀態。這個數字看起來大， 但跟整顆方塊的 4.3 × 10¹⁹ 比起來是可以數完的——事實上跟上一篇那個 2×2×2 的 3,674,160 是同一個量級（剛好 24 倍，因為 2×2×2 就是「固定一顆角」的角塊群）。 從解開狀態做一次廣度優先搜尋，114 秒之後，每一個角塊狀態離解開幾步全部在手上。

Eight corners have states. That looks large, but against the whole cube's 4.3 × 10¹⁹ it is countable — in fact it is the same order as the 3,674,160 of the 2×2×2 in the previous piece (exactly 24 times as many, since a 2×2×2 is the corner group with one corner pinned). One breadth-first sweep from solved, 114 seconds, and every corner state's exact distance is in hand.

能整個數完的關鍵是：角塊的「誰在哪」和「轉了幾度」在轉動下各自獨立—— 新 ori[M(i)] = (舊 ori[i] + 固定扭轉量) mod 3，跟哪顆角在那裡無關。 所以 40,320 × 12 和 2,187 × 12 兩張小表就推得出全部 8,800 萬個狀態的鄰居， 整個 BFS 純向量化。

What makes the full sweep cheap: for corners, "which piece is where" and "how it is twisted" transform independently under a move — new ori[M(i)] = (old ori[i] + fixed twist) mod 3, with no dependence on which corner sits there. So two small tables, 40,320 × 12 and 2,187 × 12, generate the neighbours of all 88 million states, and the whole search vectorises.

Ground truth on a 3×3×3, at last

上一篇結尾說：3×3×3 沒有正確答案，所以「解比最短解長幾步」量不出來。 角塊表把這句話改掉了一半——因為它保證不高估， 配上課本版的 A*（實作用 IDA*，見最後一節），找到的解就是可以證明的最短解。 代價是節點數隨深度暴漲，所以只做得到最短解 14 步以內。

The previous piece ended by saying a 3×3×3 has no ground truth, so "how much longer than optimal" cannot be measured. The corner table changes half of that: because it provably never over-estimates, textbook A* over it (implemented as IDA*, see the last section) returns a provably optimal solution. The cost grows steeply with depth, so this reaches optimal solutions of up to 14 moves.

深的局面：夾，但夾不緊

Deep cubes: squeezed, but not tightly

隨機打亂的方塊求不出精確最短解（節點數爆炸），但可以夾： 角塊表給下界，找到的解給上界，真正的最短解一定在中間。

A fully scrambled cube is out of reach for exact solving, but it can be bracketed: the corner table gives a lower bound, the solution found gives an upper bound, and the true optimum lies between.

這個夾子很鬆，而且鬆的原因很明確：角塊表最多只能說 14， 但隨機方塊的真實最短解在 20 上下。要夾緊，得補上另外兩張表—— Korf 用的那兩張「六條邊」。那是這個方向的下一步。

The bracket is loose, and it is loose for a clear reason: the corner table tops out at 14, while a random cube's true optimum is around 20. Tightening it needs the other two tables — Korf's two six-edge databases. That is the obvious next step.

Why A* had to go

第一版用的是這個專案原本的批次加權 A*，把 weight 設成 1、終點改成展開時判定。 它是對的，但撐不到有趣的地方：每展開一個節點要做十二次 「把 54 個位元組轉成鍵、查字典、丟進堆積」，實測每個節點約 –。 這一輪最深的那批局面一顆要展開一千八百萬個節點——用 A* 要跑一小時以上。

The first attempt reused this project's batched weighted A* with weight 1 and the goal test moved to expansion. It is correct, but it cannot reach anywhere interesting: every expanded node does twelve rounds of "turn 54 bytes into a key, hit a dictionary, push a heap" — about – per node, measured. The deepest cubes in this study expand eighteen million nodes apiece — over an hour each at that rate.

值得記住的一件事

Worth remembering

這個倍數完全沒有換演算法的「聰明」——heuristic 一模一樣，展開的節點只有更多不會更少。 差別純粹是每個節點的簿記成本。上一篇的最後一節列了五個技術挑戰， 其中第三個就是「推論加速之後，優先佇列會變成瓶頸」。這裡就是那件事的實例： 把佇列整個拿掉，事情就成了。

None of that speed-up comes from a cleverer algorithm — the heuristic is identical and IDA* expands more nodes, not fewer. The entire difference is per-node bookkeeping. The previous piece listed five technical obstacles, the third being "once inference gets faster, the priority queue becomes the bottleneck". This is that, concretely: remove the queue and the thing works.

中心塊沒有「解」這回事

There is no centre stage

先把一個很容易誤會的地方講清楚。人在手上轉方塊的時候會整顆翻來翻去，中心當然會動—— 但那不改變方塊的狀態，只是換個握法。這一頁的動作集只有六個面的順逆時針共十二種轉法， 在這個座標系下，五十四片貼紙裡有六片永遠不動，正好是六個面的正中央。 引擎驗過：五千個隨機打亂的局面，中心顏色永遠是同一組。

One thing is easy to get wrong. A person rotates the whole cube constantly while solving, so the centres do move in the room — but that changes nothing about the cube's state; it is just a re-grip. The move set on this page is the twelve quarter-turns of the six faces, and in that frame six of the fifty-four stickers never move — exactly the six face centres. Verified against the engine: over five thousand random scrambles the centre colours are always the same set.

所以 3×3×3 只有兩個階段：角、邊。（要到 4×4×4 以上，中心才會動、才需要解。） 那 Korf 為什麼有三張表？因為十二條邊一起有 12! × 2¹¹ ≈ 9,810 億個狀態，數不完； 切成兩半、每半六條，就只剩 12P6 × 2⁶ = 42,577,920 個——比角塊那張還小一半。 第三張表不是第三個階段，是同一個階段被切開來實作。

So a 3×3×3 has exactly two stages: corners, then edges. (Centres only start moving on 4×4×4 and up.) Why does Korf use three tables, then? Because twelve edges together come to 12! × 2¹¹ ≈ 981 billion states — too many to enumerate. Split into halves of six, each is 12P6 × 2⁶ = 42,577,920 — half the size of the corner table. The third table is not a third stage; it is one stage cut in two to make it fit.

All three are lower bounds: every move that solves the whole cube also moves these pieces. The maximum of three lower bounds is still a lower bound, so the optimality guarantee survives.

人先解角是對的，機器先解角是錯的

Corners first is right for a person and wrong for a machine

現在有工具可以問一個一直問不了的問題：先解角，到底繞了多遠？ 兩個階段都求到最短——第一階段沿著角塊精確表走（站在距離 d 的地方一定有鄰居是 d−1， 沿著走下去就是一條最短解，連搜尋都不用），第二階段用三張表跑 IDA*。 再跟直接求出的最短解一比，繞路就是精確值，不是估計。

Now a question that could not be asked before: how far does corners-first detour? Both stages are solved to the minimum — stage one walks the exact corner table (from a position at distance d there is always a neighbour at d−1, so following those traces a shortest path with no search at all), stage two runs IDA* on all three tables. Compare that against the direct optimum and the detour is an exact number, not an estimate.

更有意思的是第二階段為什麼難算。角塊一歸位，角塊表就永遠讀 0—— 三張表裡最強的那張直接閉嘴，只剩兩張邊塊表在說話：

More interesting is why stage two is hard. The moment the corners are home, the corner table reads zero forever. The strongest of the three tables goes silent and only the two edge tables remain:

先解角，等於把自己最好的一張表丟掉

Solving the corners first throws away your best table

人類為什麼還是先解角？因為人不是在跑 heuristic 搜尋。人用的是背下來的公式—— 一組「只動我要動的塊、其他都還原」的手順。那是完全不同的資源： 人有記憶，機器有搜尋，而先解角這個決定，剛好對記憶有利、對搜尋不利。

So why do people still solve corners first? Because a person is not running a heuristic search. A person runs memorised algorithms — sequences that move exactly the pieces they want and restore everything else. That is a different resource entirely: people have memory, machines have search, and solving corners first happens to help the former and hurt the latter.

還缺什麼

What is still missing

三張表補齊了，可解深度往前推了幾步，但離「隨便一顆都求得出最短解」還很遠—— 隨機打亂的方塊最短解大約二十步，而三張表的下界平均只有 –。中間那十步就是這個問題還沒被解決的部分。

All three tables are built and the feasible depth moved up a few moves, but "optimal solution for any cube" is still far off: a random scramble sits around twenty moves from solved, while the three-table bound averages only –. Those ten moves are the unsolved part.

而一個問題留在那裡沒動：既然精確的表跟學出來的網路各有各的毛病 （一個片面、一個不準），能不能讓網路去學「表算不出來的那一部分」？ 也就是把 max(表, 網路) 換成 表 + 學出來的殘差。那樣得到的東西不再保證是下界， 但這一頁已經量過：不保證下界，換來的是可解深度。那個取捨值不值，是下一篇的事。

One question is left untouched: since the exact tables and the learned network fail in different ways — one is partial, the other inaccurate — could the network be trained on what the tables cannot see? That is, replace max(table, network) with table plus a learned residual. The result would no longer be a guaranteed lower bound — but this page already measured that trade: giving up the guarantee buys reachable depth. Whether it is worth it is the next piece.
